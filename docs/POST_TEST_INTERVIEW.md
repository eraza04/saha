# Saha retrospective questions — planned use

Record participant code, session ID, build and condition on the answer sheet. Do not enter names or real health details in the app. Ask these questions after timing has stopped. This is a script, not a report of findings.

## After each task

1. What were you trying to do?
2. Was anything unclear or unexpected?
3. If you changed direction or repeated an action, what led you to do that?

Use neutral follow-ups such as “Could you tell me more?” Avoid suggesting that a control or gesture should have been easier. Do not reinterpret an incorrect task response as successful merely because the participant liked the interface. Record explanations separately from timed performance.

## After each condition

Administer the standard, unchanged 10-item System Usability Scale (SUS) using the agreed language version and five-point response scale. Record all item responses, missing answers and reading assistance. Do not coach responses. The source and scoring procedure are linked in [EXPERIMENTAL_PLAN.md](EXPERIMENTAL_PLAN.md). Saha does not contain a questionnaire collector; use a separate coded paper or course-approved form. Do not label these interview questions as a substitute SUS instrument.

Then ask:

1. Which parts felt easy, and which needed effort?
2. How did the text, contrast and space between controls feel?
3. How did you decide where to go next?
4. What did “Record taken” mean to you? What changed when a reminder was postponed?
5. What did you expect Cancel or Correct record to do?
6. Was there a point where you wanted help?

After C, also ask: “Did you choose to use swipes? Why or why not?” and “How did swipes and buttons compare for you?” Accept non-use without suggesting it was a mistake. Ask about attempted swipes without assuming all were recognized.

## Final comparison interview

1. Which version would you choose for these fictional tasks, if any? Why?
2. Did your preferred version differ from the one in which you felt most accurate or confident?
3. Were any comfort choices especially useful or unhelpful?
4. Would you choose buttons, optional swipes, or both? In what situations?
5. Was it clear what was recorded, what could be corrected, and what was only a simulation?
6. What would you change first?
7. Is there anything else you would like us to know?

Note the condition order when interpreting preferences, and retain negative or mixed views. Ask permission under the agreed consent procedure before quoting comments. Do not collect personal medicine histories, make clinical recommendations, or claim that preferences establish health benefit.
